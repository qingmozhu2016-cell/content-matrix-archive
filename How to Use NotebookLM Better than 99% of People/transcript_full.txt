== SOURCE ANALYSIS PROMPTS / 来源分析提示词 ==

[Matt Wolfe]: Something I do anytime I'm using this for research and deep diving into a topic is running a few prompts to check how good my sources are. I said NotebookLM is amazing for reducing hallucinations, but that doesn't help you if you have poor or biased sources.

So I start with this prompt: "Looking only at the sources in this notebook, identify any areas where the sources disagree with each other and any clear contradictions or conflicting claims." If I have a diverse range of sources and they all mostly agree, then I'm pretty comfortable moving forward with that information. If they don't agree, this helps pinpoint any areas they may either be wrong or just require nuance.

I run that on every research notebook. I'll save that as a note and then delete the chat history and run prompt number two: "Based on these sources, what important questions or subtopics about AI water use are missing or barely covered? List the biggest gaps that would need to be filled to really understand this topic well. Do not invent details. Just describe what is missing." Sometimes your sources are incomplete, and you can't know what angles are missing on a new topic. This prompt helps surface those blind spots.

And I'm going to save that as a note and start the chat over with this prompt: "Are there any contrarian, alternative, or lesser-known viewpoints on AI water use that are likely not represented in these sources? Describe those possible viewpoints at a high level and suggest what kinds of sources I would need to look for to find them." This one's especially useful for controversial or evolving topics. The consensus view isn't always the correct one. This helps identify alternative viewpoints you might be missing.

For any serious research project, I use one or more of these prompts before diving deep.

== OTHER USE CASES / 其他使用场景 ==

[Matt Wolfe]: Research and learning is what most people use NotebookLM for. But I don't think it crosses most people's minds how flexible this tool actually is. So I'm gonna run through some unique use cases:

1. A tech manual database or homeowner's binder — upload owner's manuals for every product you own. Now anytime something breaks, you have every manual searchable in your pocket.

2. Personal journal analysis — upload all your entries. Ask it to identify blind spots, recurring themes, patterns. You could connect it to a Gem and give it instructions to act as a cognitive behavioral therapist.

3. A fitness and nutrition coach — upload your workout logs, meal plans, and fitness advice. Ask it to analyze your progress, suggest routines, or create grocery lists.

4. Meeting notes archive — all your work meetings in one searchable place. Track progress, identify recurring pain points.

5. Debate prep — upload research on both sides of a topic, then use the debate feature in audio overview to hear arguments and counterarguments.

6. A recipe book — upload your family recipes, cooking notes, and dietary restrictions.

7. A book companion — upload a book (or audiobook transcript) and use NotebookLM as a reading companion, study guide, or discussion partner.

8. A travel planner — upload travel guides, articles, and itineraries. Get personalized recommendations.

9. Language learning — upload texts in a target language. Use flashcards, quizzes, and chat to practice.

10. A personal CRM — upload contact notes, meeting summaries, and relationship histories.
